# Writing for this blog

Tania writes posts for two reasons:

1. **To show her work and skills.** She programs out of passion, not only for money: it's a way of life. That should come through in the writing.
2. **To help others** learn from her experience: the lessons, and the solutions she found to problems along the way.

Every paragraph should serve one of the two. Leave out:

- details that don't help the reader or show something about the work;
- step-by-step accounts that don't teach anything;
- filler and "AI prose": no hype, no cute personification, no fluffy phrasing. She would never write those herself.

How she writes: simple, clear, plain words, short sentences, first person, like talking to someone. Say why, not only what, and in her own reasons. Call things by their plain name (a notification, not "a bark"). Quote her own messages as she wrote them (typos fixed). English posts have no Greek text.

What goes in a post:

- **Her real process.** Describe what she actually did, not what seems likely. If you don't know how she did something, ask.
- **The result for her, not how it works inside.** "The CV on my website stays generic, and each tailored version stays in its application's doc", not which version gets approved or where files are copied. Keep implementation detail only when it teaches the reader something.
- **No attention on AI writing her content.** Leave out cover letters, application answers or anything about the AI writing in her voice.
- **A point the reader can use in every section.** Cut details that only matter to her own setup, and anything an earlier post already said. If a section's point isn't clear, rewrite it around the problem, what changed and the benefit, or drop it.
- **Her reasons in her words.** When she gives the why, use it nearly as she said it.

When suggesting changes to a post, give a list of before → after.
